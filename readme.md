Abstract: To address the uncertainties of renewable energy and load within microgrids, a two-stage robust optimization model with a min-max-min structure was established. This model can generate the least operational cost scheduling scheme under the most adverse scenarios. The model considers operational constraints and coordinated control for storage, demand-side loads, and controllable distributed power sources, and introduces uncertainty adjustment parameters to flexibly adjust the conservativeness of the scheduling scheme. Based on column generation algorithms and strong duality theory, the original problem can be decomposed into a principal problem with mixed integer linear characteristics and a subproblem for alternating solution, thereby obtaining the optimal solution of the original problem. Finally, simulation analysis validated the effectiveness of the established model and solving algorithm. At the same time, the boundary conditions for scheduling storage in microgrids under time-of-use pricing mechanisms were provided, which can serve as a reference for investors in microgrids and power grid operators in designing incentive mechanisms.

This paper establishes an economic dispatch model for microgrids considering renewable distributed generation and load uncertainties based on a two-stage robust optimization method. The analysis results indicate: 1) The proposed model takes into account the uncertainties of renewable distributed generation and load, and through solving the two-stage robust optimization model, the microgrid can obtain the least operational cost under the "worst" scenario; 2) By adjusting the uncertainty adjustment parameters, the flexibility of the microgrid's optimization scheme can be adjusted, which is beneficial for the microgrid operator to make reasonable choices between operational costs and operational risks; 3) The advantage of the robust optimization method over deterministic optimization methods becomes more significant with increasing prediction errors, and the obtained day-ahead dispatch plan has stronger robustness and the ability to resist real-time market price fluctuation risks; 4) Under the time-of-use pricing mechanism, the microgrid's dispatch plan for energy storage depends on the relationship between the peak-valley electricity price difference and the unit charging/discharging cost of energy storage, and this conclusion can provide reference for microgrid investors in planning energy storage, as well as help power distribution network operators design reasonable incentive mechanisms. 🎉3 References [1] Liu Yixin, Guo Li, Wang Chengshan.
